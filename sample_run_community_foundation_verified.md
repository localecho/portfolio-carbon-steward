## **The Maple Street Community Foundation finances 171.2 tCO2e per year (range: 129.7–217.0 tCO2e, Scope 1+2 basis), covering 100% of the $2.4 million portfolio.**

### **What that means**

That's equivalent to the annual emissions from 37 passenger cars, or roughly 10.7 average American personal carbon footprints. The foundation sits near the middle of what a typical 401(k) investor would carry: the Vanguard target-date fund range for $2.4 million is 151–190 tCO2e per year, depending on the fund's stock-bond mix. The band reflects the uncertainty baked into the underlying carbon intensity estimates across all asset classes; it is not a statistical confidence interval but the spread of those inputs themselves.

Fossil fuels, though only 4% of the allocation, contribute 13% of the footprint. International developed stocks have higher average intensity (90 tCO2e per $1M) than US equities (60), driven by energy-heavy industries in European portfolios.

### **Options the committee could consider**

**1. Exit the 4% fossil-fuel position entirely (all $96,000 into clean energy)**

Moving all of XLE into clean energy would save **20.0 tCO2e per year**, a **11.7% reduction**, bringing the total to 151.2 tCO2e. This move is capped by the holding's size; it exhausts the fossil-fuel sleeve. Alternates: real estate (18.0 tonnes saved), US bonds (17.1), or US equities (16.6).

**2. Reduce emerging markets by 4.5 points (all $108,000 into clean energy)**

Moving the emerging-markets position to clean energy would save **17.8 tCO2e per year**, a **10.4% reduction**, to 153.4 tCO2e. Emerging markets carry higher-than-average intensity (190 tCO2e per $1M) due to coal and oil exposure. Alternates: real estate (15.7 tonnes saved), US bonds (14.6), or US equities (14.0).

**3. Reduce int'l developed by up to 10 points ($240,000 into clean energy)**

Moving 10 percentage points of the international-developed position would save **15.6 tCO2e per year**, a **9.1% reduction**, to 155.6 tCO2e. This is the largest move under the 10-point threshold and does not exhaust that sleeve.

**Important caveat (carbon-only):** These options are carbon-only analysis and do not model return, risk, fees, liquidity or fiduciary constraints — a committee should weigh these options with its investment advisor or policy statement before acting.

### **How sure is this**

**Scope basis:** Scope 1+2 (direct operations and purchased energy). Every asset class is on the same basis, so fossil fuels and clean energy compare like for like.

**Band:** The 129.7–217.0 tCO2e range reflects the low and high estimates for each asset class held at the same weights—the spread of the underlying carbon intensity inputs, not a statistical confidence interval.

**Asset-class limitation:** These are class-average intensities (e.g., "US equities" = S&P 500 average), not fund-level or company-level data. The model cannot tell two funds within the same sleeve apart.

**Fossil-fuels note:** The often-quoted 700 tCO2e per $1M is Scope 1+2+3; on the Scope 1+2 basis here it is 233, so fossil fuels are ~9× as carbon-intensive as clean energy, not 28×.
